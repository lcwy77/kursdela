The board of Kazatomprom, the world's largest producer of natural uranium, has called an extraordinary shareholders' meeting with a single item: whether to approve a contract to supply U3O8 uranium concentrate to Uranium One Group, which is owned by Rosatom. The vote will be held by absentee ballot. The shareholder register will be fixed on November 17, ballots will be sent out from November 25 and accepted until 6 p.m. on December 9, and the results will be announced on December 10. The meeting is quorate if holders of more than half of the shares take part.

**Why "again."** Shareholders have already considered this deal. At an extraordinary meeting whose results were announced on October 7, 2026, the contract failed: a simple majority was required, and 88.34% of voting shares abstained. Some 5.39% voted in favor and 6.27% against. The deal is classified as a major related-party transaction, so the decision rests with shareholders.

**What was approved the same day.** At the same meeting, shareholders overwhelmingly backed a spot contract to supply uranium concentrate to China's SNURDC: 88.63% in favor, 6.49% against, 4.88% abstaining. The representative of the Samruk-Kazyna fund on the board also changed: Zhandos Kairgeldy was elected in place of Elzhas Otyshiev. Holders of 99.21% of voting shares took part in the October vote.

**Who the buyer is.** Uranium One Group belongs to Rosatom. The company produces about 4,800 tonnes of natural uranium a year and supplies it to utilities in Europe, North America and Asia. In Kazakhstan, it develops the Budenovskoye deposit together with Kazatomprom and, as Kursiv reports, has recently been selling Kazakh assets to Chinese investors. Rosatom itself is not under Western sanctions, but restrictions have been imposed on a number of its subsidiaries and top managers. At the same time, Rosatom leads the consortium building Kazakhstan's first nuclear power plant.

**What it means.** The reasons shareholders abstained in October have not been made public, and the board has not commented on the repeat vote. The December 10 result will show whether the world's largest uranium supplier is prepared to keep its Russian sales channel on a par with its Chinese one, while the Russian side scales back its presence in Kazakhstan's uranium business.

**What comes next.** Kazatomprom will publish the results on December 10. This page will be updated.
