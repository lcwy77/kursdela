**VK is seeking more than €31 million from Apple, plus interest, over money the U.S. company collected through the App Store from users of VK services. This is a separate dispute from the case over the removal of its apps. But the larger question goes beyond the amount claimed: why have Russian banks tried to return to Apple's store under new names, while VK has pursued litigation? U.S. court filings suggest that the answer may have less to do with the company's preference than with its ability to publish apps at all.**

On November 20, VK announced a new lawsuit against Apple. According to the holding's press office, Apple stopped transferring funds collected through the App Store from users of VK apps in 2025. VK says the payments were halted without explanation or notice. It is seeking more than €31 million, as well as interest for the use of the money.

The defendants include Apple Inc., Ireland-based Apple Distribution International and Russia's Apple Rus LLC. The claim was registered with the Moscow Arbitration Court on November 19 under case number A40-410082/2026. The court docket, however, lists the claim amount as 10 million rubles, while VK has announced a demand of more than €31 million. No public explanation for the discrepancy was available at the time of publication. The court had not yet accepted the claim for proceedings.

One distinction matters: the €31 million figure comes from VK. It is not an amount awarded or confirmed by a court. The company has not disclosed the period covered by the alleged arrears or published a detailed calculation.

## Payments for purchases, not compensation for app removals

The new claim concerns proceeds from purchases made by users inside VK apps. It is not the same claim as the dispute over the removal of the company's services from the App Store.

Under Apple's standard model, the company processes payments for apps and in-app purchases, deducts its commission and applicable taxes, and transfers the remainder to the developer. Apple's developer documentation says that, subject to the relevant requirements, payments are made within 45 days after the end of the fiscal month in which the transaction took place.

VK says the transfers stopped in 2025, before the mass removal of its apps from the App Store in June 2026. The two disputes therefore have different timelines and legal grounds: one concerns money from purchases, the other access to Apple's platform and the distribution of apps.

Apple has not publicly set out its position on the new payment claim. Without its response and the underlying documents, it is not possible to establish why the transfers were stopped or what sums, if any, are due.

## How VK lost access to the App Store

The first major product from the holding to disappear from Apple's store was MAX, on June 3, 2026. On June 25, Apple removed a wider group of VK apps, including VKontakte, Odnoklassniki, VK Video, VK Music, VK Messenger, Zen, Mail services and other products.

Apps already installed on users' devices continued to work, but users could no longer download them again or receive updates. VK also warned of problems with push notifications.

The public explanations from the two sides differed. VK said it was not under sanctions and that its apps had been removed without warning. Apple told the BBC that it had acted to comply with sanctions rules, without specifying which restrictions it meant.

Apple gave a more detailed account in U.S. court filings. The company said that, during a review, it had identified concerns about the ownership and control of VK and its parent structure by people on sanctions lists. According to Apple's account, it suspended VK's participation in the Apple Developer Program on June 23 and removed the holding's apps. Apple also said it had emailed the designated owner of VK's developer account.

That is Apple's position as presented in court documents. It is not a judicial finding that VK violated sanctions. The holding has disputed the grounds for removing its apps.

## The banks' workaround: a new name, a short life

After their official apps were removed from the App Store, Russian banks repeatedly tried to return with products carrying neutral names and descriptions.

In June 2026, Sber released Family Online, an app that contained banking functions. T-Bank introduced Toastmas, nominally an event-planning app. Both appeared in the App Store on June 23 and became unavailable for download the following day.

Earlier, VTB had returned under the name Sirius. The app was presented as a timer for working with the Pomodoro technique. Such products can briefly restore some familiar functions for customers, but they do not guarantee a lasting presence. Apple can identify an app's connection to a previously removed service and take it down.

This is not a full return to the App Store. Users get a temporary access channel, while the developer has to keep looking for ways to distribute the app and warn customers that it may disappear.

## Why doesn't VK do the same?

We found no public statement in which VK says it has categorically rejected the idea of disguising its apps. It would therefore be too strong to claim that the holding simply does not want to follow the banks' example.

The U.S. court case points to a technical difference. According to Apple, on June 23, 2026, it suspended VK's participation in the Apple Developer Program. That is more than the removal of an individual app from the store: the company lost access to its own developer account. Merely changing an app's name and icon does not remove that restriction.

The banks' cases show that apps with new names can appear in the store for a short time. But public information does not establish that all banks used the same publishing mechanism, or that VK could reproduce it on the same terms. Publishing through a third-party developer would not guarantee that Apple could not identify the connection to the original developer.

There is also a question of scale. A bank may need to restore one customer-facing service to provide access to accounts, transfers and payments. VK has to deal with an entire ecosystem: a social network, video, music, email, messaging and other products. A temporary app under another name cannot replace a full infrastructure for updates, notifications and support for tens of millions of users.

The more precise conclusion is not that VK has demonstrably refused to disguise its apps. It is that the banks' workaround is neither a simple nor a guaranteed solution for the holding. VK has not publicly said whether it considered this option.

## MAX: a separate strand of the dispute

MAX disappeared from the App Store on June 3, almost three weeks before the main group of VK apps was removed. Apple also said it was acting to comply with sanctions rules. Users who already had the messenger installed could continue using it, but faced a lack of updates and push notifications.

On July 13, 2026, the Council of the European Union added VK and its subsidiary Communication Platform, which is responsible for developing and managing MAX, to its sanctions list. The EU document identifies the holding as the messenger developer's parent company.

The chronology is important. The EU decision came after MAX and the VK apps were removed in June. The July listing therefore could not have been the original reason for Apple's June decision. It did, however, add another layer of sanctions restrictions and made the prospects for the holding's products on international platforms more complicated.

The issue is particularly sensitive for VK because MAX is promoted as a national digital platform and is being integrated with Russian government services. Its distribution on Apple devices remains restricted.

## The legal fight moves to California

VK's first lawsuit against Apple in the Moscow Arbitration Court was accepted for proceedings on September 3, 2026. The company asked the court to order the return of its apps to the App Store, restore their functionality, including push notifications, and impose a penalty of 58 million rubles for each day of non-compliance with a possible court ruling.

Apple went to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California. It relied on the Apple Developer Program License Agreement, which VK accepted when it joined the developer program. The agreement says that disputes between the parties related to the agreement and their relationship with Apple should be heard in courts in the Northern District of California.

On November 6, the U.S. court issued a preliminary injunction in Apple's favour. It ordered VK to seek a stay of the Moscow proceedings and barred the company from taking further steps in certain disputes with Apple outside the California jurisdiction. The court noted that VK had not appeared at the U.S. hearing and had not submitted objections to Apple's request for the preliminary injunction.

The wording of the order reaches beyond the case already under way. It also covers certain other current or future disputes related to the developer agreement, Apple's software or the parties' relationship. The new €31 million payment claim could therefore trigger a separate dispute over where it may be heard. At the time of publication, however, no court had ruled specifically on the fate of this new claim.

## What happens next

The VK–Apple conflict now has at least three distinct dimensions.

The first is money. VK is seeking more than €31 million, but the court has not yet accepted the new claim, and the amount listed in the docket differs from the company's announcement.

The second is access to the platform. The suspension of VK's developer account and the removal of its apps mean the problem cannot be solved simply by renaming an app. The banks' cases show that temporary workarounds are possible, but unstable. They do not restore full access to the App Store.

The third is jurisdiction. Apple has obtained a preliminary injunction in a U.S. court, while VK continues to pursue its interests in Russia. The next question is whether the U.S. order will also affect the new payment dispute.

The €31 million claim is substantial, but money is not the only asset at stake. For VK, it is access to iPhone users. For Apple, it is control over its platform and compliance with sanctions requirements. Until the two companies find a way to resolve the conflict, users remain caught between their competing legal strategies.
