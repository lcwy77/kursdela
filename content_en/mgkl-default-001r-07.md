PAO MGKL, formerly the pawnbroker Mosgorlombard, has gone into its first full default. The company did not pay a 24.66 million ruble coupon on its 001R-07 bonds, and on November 16, according to the Moscow Exchange, the technical default became a full default, RBC Investments reported. The technical default on this issue occurred on October 29.

It was not the only one. From November 3 to 11, the company also missed coupon payments on four more issues: 001RS-02 (20.1 million rubles), 001R-06 (9.45 million), 001PS-03 (13.5 million) and 001PS-01 (19.7 million). MGKL has 12 bond issues outstanding and 6.5 billion rubles in public debt, according to Cbonds. The bonds trade at discounts of 4% to 28% to par.

On November 2, the rating agency NRA downgraded the company from BB|ru| to C|ru| with "under watch" status.

MGKL had promised an official statement answering investors' main questions on November 16. At the time of publication it had not appeared. In recent messages, the company said it "continues work on settling the debts of its subsidiaries," that PAO MGKL has no tax arrears, and that its financial statements, including goodwill, were prepared and audited in line with approved procedures.

Questions about its accounts arose in the summer. In late August, the Bondholders Association asked the governor of the Bank of Russia to review the quality of MGKL's audit, citing opaque acquisitions. In its half-year accounts, MGKL reported buying OOO Zvezda for 940 million rubles, of which 760 million was goodwill.

**Update, November 17, 2026. The statement arrives, with a raider-takeover theory.** On the evening of November 16, MGKL finally issued the promised statement. The company confirmed that it had not paid the coupon and pledged to draw up and present to investors a debt restructuring program within two months, with specific deadlines and a procedure for meeting the deferred obligations.

In the same statement, management did not rule out that the situation was caused by "external pressure with signs of an attempted raider takeover." The company reported simultaneous cyberattacks on its IT infrastructure and abnormal exchange volatility, with trading volume in its securities up to 100 times the historical average, and said it would turn to supervisory and law enforcement authorities. MGKL did not say whom it suspects. "The MGKL group continues to operate and serve its clients," the statement said. Management and the board, according to the company, retain control of the group.

MGKL shares fell almost 20% on November 16, to 0.301 rubles, before paring losses to 5.6% by the evening. Since November 13, its securities have been in the Moscow Exchange's higher investment risk segment. In late October, tax authorities suspended operations on the accounts of MGKL and eight of its 11 subsidiaries; the block on the parent company was lifted, but it remains in place for most of the subsidiaries.
