On the morning of November 13, a black Toyota Fortuner pulled out of a courtyard on Kirovogradskaya Street in Moscow's Chertanovo district. It had gone about two hundred meters when an explosive device went off beneath it.

At the wheel was Sergei Zavorotny, former press secretary and adviser to ex-Ukrainian prime minister Mykola Azarov. According to media reports, he lost an arm and suffered a severe head injury; he is in intensive care on a ventilator. Another man was wounded in the legs and hit by shrapnel. The Investigative Committee opened a case of attempted murder committed by a means dangerous to the public and by a group of persons.

According to media reports, a bomb of about 300 grams was placed under the driver's seat and was probably detonated remotely. An early version blamed a faulty gas cylinder, but investigators opened the case specifically as attempted murder. Azarov said he had no doubt of a "Ukrainian trace." Investigators have not officially named who ordered the attack.

## A man from the prime minister's office

Zavorotny is an international journalist with a doctorate in history. For about ten years he was a Komsomolskaya Pravda correspondent in Latin America, and in 2003–2004 he headed the press service of the Rodina faction in the State Duma. From 2009 he worked as aide and press secretary to Azarov, who led Ukraine's government in 2010–2014 under Viktor Yanukovych.

After the Maidan, Zavorotny followed Azarov to Moscow. In the summer of 2015 he was press secretary of the "Committee for the Salvation of Ukraine," which Azarov presented as an alternative to the authorities in Kyiv. He later ran the website Antifashist, and in 2018 co-wrote with Oleksiy Berdnikov a book about Yanukovych's last months in power.

## Two blasts in two days

The Chertanovo bombing was the second in a day. At about 7 a.m. on November 12, in Shushary outside St. Petersburg, a car belonging to an Air Force colonel exploded. According to preliminary reports, he was chief navigator of the 6th Air Force and Air Defense Army. He was killed.

The bomb was placed under the car at about 1 a.m.; the car traveled about three hundred meters. According to Fontanka, the device was brought into the Leningrad region by balloon from the Baltic states. The suspect, a 31-year-old St. Petersburg resident, flew to Istanbul overnight and then on to Chisinau.

That same morning, the plane carrying US special envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner landed at Vnukovo. That afternoon Vladimir Putin talked with them in the Kremlin for more than two hours. Yuri Ushakov called the conversation "substantive, constructive, frank and useful": the sides are agreeing on the largest prisoner exchange, and Moscow is ready to discuss a new European security architecture. The envoys then met Volodymyr Zelensky in Dublin, and Kushner wrote of "significant progress." The next morning, Zavorotny's car was blown up in Moscow.

## From Dugina to Shushary

Over the past four years, bombings and assassination attempts in Russia have become almost routine:

- **August 20, 2022.** Darya Dugina's car is blown up outside Moscow; the journalist is killed. The FSB blames Ukrainian intelligence.
- **April 2, 2023.** Military blogger Vladlen Tatarsky is killed by a bomb in a St. Petersburg café. Darya Trepova, who handed him a statuette containing the bomb, is sentenced to 27 years.
- **May 6, 2023.** Writer Zakhar Prilepin's car is blown up in the Nizhny Novgorod region. His driver is killed; Prilepin is wounded.
- **December 17, 2024.** General Igor Kirillov, head of the radiation, chemical and biological protection troops, is killed outside his building in Moscow by a bomb hidden in a scooter. A source in Ukraine's SBU told media the service was behind it.
- **February 3, 2025.** Armen Sarkisyan, founder of the Arbat battalion, is killed by a blast in Moscow's Alye Parusa residential complex.
- **April 25, 2025.** A car bomb in Balashikha kills General Yaroslav Moskalik, deputy head of the General Staff's Main Operations Directorate. The Foreign Ministry says there are grounds to believe Ukrainian intelligence was involved.
- **December 22, 2025.** A bomb under a car on Yasenevaya Street in Moscow kills Lieutenant General Fanil Sarvarov, head of the General Staff's operational training directorate.
- **August 13, 2026.** In Sevastopol, a blast kills Robert Shageev, a former Ukrainian submarine commander who sided with Russia in 2014.
- **November 12, 2026.** Shushary, an Air Force colonel.
- **November 13, 2026.** Moscow, Sergei Zavorotny.

## Too many coincidences

Moskalik was killed on the morning of April 25, 2025 — the day Witkoff flew to Moscow and spent more than three hours with Putin. Ushakov said then that the positions of Moscow and Washington had converged on a number of issues. Andrei Kolesnik, a member of the Duma's defense committee, called the bombing an attempt to derail the talks.

Sarvarov was killed on December 22, 2025, a day after Kirill Dmitriev wrapped up talks with Witkoff and Kushner in Miami, which he called "constructive."

Now Shushary and Chertanovo. Both blasts came in exactly the hours when the American envoys were flying from Abu Dhabi to Moscow, and from Moscow to Dublin.

Investigators have not yet named who ordered the attacks. But if these are coincidences, they are remarkably precise. Each time the talks move forward, a bomb goes off in Russia. It looks less like revenge on individuals than like a provocation meant to make Moscow walk away from the table.

On Thursday, Ushakov spoke of the largest prisoner exchange of the entire conflict. On Friday, Zavorotny was rushed into intensive care.
