The Moscow Arbitration Court has dismissed a claim by Basalt SPO against the Ministry of Digital Development, which refused to add the office suite "Svobodny Ofis" (Free Office) to the unified register of Russian software. The reasoning was published on October 8, 2026. Published reports do not give the case number, and the company has not yet announced an appeal.

**What the dispute is about.** Svobodny Ofis is a package of seven programs, five of which were developed by third parties under open licenses. It is built on LibreOffice, the free office suite developed by the international community The Document Foundation. In the spring, the expert council at the Digital Ministry voted against adding the suite to the register, the ministry refused, and in May Basalt went to court.

The question is broader than one product: how much original code must an open-source-based build contain to count as Russian? The register holds more than 26,000 programs, and inclusion brings tax breaks and access to public procurement.

**What the court decided.** As Kommersant and CNews summarize the ruling, the court classified the product as a "set of programs" rather than a single "software package," and therefore assessed each component separately. Basalt does not hold the rights to the key component, LibreOffice. The court found its changes to it — Russian translation, dictionaries, an electronic signature plug-in — insufficient. In addition, in the court's view, the components contact foreign servers for updates, and the terms of open licenses restrict commercialization, which does not comply with certain provisions of the register rules.

**The Digital Ministry's position.** The ministry told Kommersant that it does not restrict the inclusion of open-license software in the register: "However, such a product must be modified by a Russian developer." In the expert council's assessment, Svobodny Ofis is a build in which open-source components, LibreOffice in particular, are "presented without modifications."

**Basalt's position.** Alexei Smirnov, the company's co-founder and chairman of the board, calls the claim about foreign updates unfounded: according to him, "updates come only from the company's server and are voluntary." He points out that the register rules do not require exclusive rights to all components of a composite product, that free licenses permit sales, and that the rules do not set the amount of modification required. The company, he says, made changes, including integrating Russian cryptography.

Back in the spring, after the expert council's negative opinion, Smirnov told ComNews that if the current requirements are insufficient, new ones should be introduced officially rather than applied informally. He also noted that the register already contains many products with GPL-licensed components.

**Who else is involved.** Basalt SPO, the developer of the Alt operating systems, has been partly owned by Rostelecom since November 2025. Back in January, Smirnov named the office software developers MyOffice and R7 as his main opponents. MyOffice told ComNews in the spring that it is individuals, not companies, who vote on the expert council, and that products almost entirely repeating LibreOffice's code carry risks: their vendor is slower to close vulnerabilities, and their development depends on the international community. R7 declined to comment.

**A precedent.** Something similar has happened before. In 2019, the office suite AlterOffice was removed from the register as a LibreOffice clone. In 2020, a court found that unlawful, and the product was reinstated.

**What comes next.** The first-instance ruling can be appealed. Expert Igor Bederov told ComNews that the Digital Ministry is preparing stricter rules for products based on free code. Until the rules are formalized, the bar for open-source builds is in effect set by the expert council and the courts. This page will be updated if Basalt files an appeal or the Digital Ministry approves new requirements.
