An arbitral tribunal under the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) has found the unilateral termination of the contract to build the Hanhikivi-1 nuclear power plant in Finland unlawful, Rosatom announced on October 10, 2026. The text of the award has not been published, and the parties have not disclosed the case number.

According to the state corporation, the tribunal also found that the project was progressing at the time of termination and that Rosatom's companies had made progress toward obtaining a construction license.

The award does not grant any money. It establishes that the contract was terminated unlawfully. How much the Finnish side must pay for that will be determined separately.

**What the dispute is about.** In 2013, Finland's Fennovoima ordered a single 1,200-megawatt unit from Rosatom for a site in the north of the country. The project was valued at €7–7.5 billion, and the plant was to supply about 10% of Finland's electricity. In May 2022, Fennovoima terminated the contract with the contractor, RAOS Project.

**Fennovoima's position.** The company attributed the termination to significant delays and the contractor's inability to complete the project, as well as to the changed political situation. Fennovoima chief executive Joachim Specht said Rosatom had warned in early 2022 that the schedule could slip by several years. Published reports contain no comment from the Finnish side on the new award.

**Rosatom's position.** The state corporation considers the termination unfounded. Rosatom chief executive Alexei Likhachev: "Today's victory is important, but not final. We will continue to prove we are right on the Finnish project in courts of various instances." He said it was the second decision in the company's favor: in December 2022, a dispute adjudication board had found the termination unlawful.

**The amounts.** Rosatom has filed six claims totaling about €3 billion, including compensation for the value of its Fennovoima shares: after a share issue in late 2022, the state corporation's stake in the company fell from 34% to 3%. Fennovoima, for its part, is seeking the return of its advance payment, €1.75 billion according to Interfax. Rosatom says the final amounts will be determined once all the proceedings are complete.

**A second front.** The Moscow Arbitration Court is hearing a 227.8 billion ruble claim by Rosatom units against Fennovoima's shareholders, Finland's Fortum and Outokumpu. The next hearing is scheduled for November 26, 2026. In February 2025, an international tribunal declined to hear the claims against Outokumpu, citing lack of jurisdiction.

**What comes next.** Alexander Grebelsky, managing partner of the law firm Grebelsky & Partners, told Kommersant: "So far the tribunal has established the basis for liability — that the termination of the EPC contract was unlawful." In his view, damages and interest will be determined at a separate stage.

There is also the question of whom to collect from. Andrei Gusev, senior partner at the law firm Nordic Star, notes that Fennovoima's equity was negative according to its 2025 figures, and shareholders are not automatically liable for its debts. That also explains the Moscow claim brought directly against Fortum and Outokumpu.

For now, the award is known only from one side's account. This page will be updated when Fennovoima comments or a decision on the amount is made.
