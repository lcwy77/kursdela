U.S. fund Carlyle is no longer in active talks to buy LUKOIL's foreign business. A Carlyle representative confirmed this to Reuters and declined further comment.

The parties signed an agreement in January 2026. The portfolio, which excluded the Kazakh assets, was valued at about $22 billion. The deal needed approval from the U.S. Treasury, which never came, and the agreement lapsed at the end of July. That became known from the financial statements of LUKOIL International.

It is the second buyer to fall through. LUKOIL announced the sale in October 2025, after U.S. sanctions. The first bidder was the trader Gunvor, and Washington did not approve its deal either. Now, according to Reuters, at least ten companies and investors are interested, including Exxon Mobil. LUKOIL itself speaks of "intensive negotiations." The license for those talks runs until October 22.

Both times there was a buyer and a price; what was missing was a single signature. A $22 billion asset is being sold not on the market but in Washington offices. According to The New York Times, its fate is being discussed as part of a package with a settlement in Ukraine; no one has officially confirmed this.

**Update, November 16, 2026. Deadline: November 21.** On October 21, a day before the previous authorization expired, the U.S. Treasury extended OFAC General License 131K for LUKOIL International GmbH by one month. Negotiations on the sale of the foreign business and the signing of contingent contracts are now permitted until November 21. The sale itself and any transfer of shares still require separate OFAC authorization. Separately, the license covering LUKOIL's foreign filling stations has been extended to April 24, 2027.

According to The New York Times, the leading bidder is a group led by U.S. investor Todd Boehly, in which investors from the UAE and Qatar would hold more than half of the equity and the U.S. government would receive about 15% through the Development Finance Corporation. This has not been officially confirmed. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov called the deal commercial and declined to discuss it publicly. The license expires in five days; no deal signing has been reported.
