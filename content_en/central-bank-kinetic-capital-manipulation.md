The Bank of Russia has found market manipulation by Vadim Solomakhin, chief executive and beneficial owner of the asset manager Kinetic Capital. The company is a licensed securities market participant and manages the closed-end mutual fund Kinetic Portfolio.

**The scheme.** According to the regulator, in the first quarter of 2026 Solomakhin traded between his personal brokerage account and the account of Kinetic Capital through which the fund was managed. He would first buy or sell securities at the market price and then place reverse orders at a price more favorable to himself. Using his position, he entered matching orders from the company's account that briefly pushed market prices off course. The central bank counted more than 120 such trades.

**Which securities.** Shares of Sberbank, Sovcombank and Surgutneftegas, and federal loan bonds (OFZ).

**The result.** According to the central bank, the company suffered losses and Solomakhin made a profit. The regulator did not disclose the amounts.

**The measures.** The Bank of Russia ordered Solomakhin to prevent such violations, suspended operations on his brokerage accounts and passed the materials to law enforcement. Published reports contain no comment from Solomakhin or Kinetic Capital.

**Context.** It is not the only such case in recent months. In late August 2026, the central bank found manipulation of OGK-2 shares: two private investors were held administratively liable, and the Moscow Exchange and brokers were ordered to suspend operations on their trading accounts.

**Why it matters.** Asset management and mutual funds rest on the premise that the manager acts in clients' interests. A unitholder does not see every trade the fund makes. If the manager is also on the other side of the trade, the conflict of interest turns into a direct loss for investors. The central bank's findings are a regulator's decision, not a court verdict; further assessment is up to law enforcement.
